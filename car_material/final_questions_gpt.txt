SPECIES CONCEPTS

Biological Species Concept
	•	Definition: A species is a group of actually or potentially interbreeding individuals that are reproductively isolated from others
	•	Key idea: Can they mate and produce viable offspring?
	•	Works well for: sexually reproducing organisms
	•	Fails for: asexual organisms, fossils, ring species
	•	Example: Lions vs tigers (can hybridize but still considered separate)

⸻

Morphological Species Concept
	•	Definition: Species are defined by physical traits (appearance)
	•	Key idea: Looks similar = same species
	•	Problem: Convergent evolution can trick you
	•	Works for: fossils, when no genetic data available
	•	Example: Fossils classified by bone structure

⸻

Phylogenetic (Lineage) Species Concept
	•	Definition: Species are groups that share a common ancestor and are genetically distinct
	•	Key idea: Based on evolutionary history + DNA
	•	Works for: all organisms (even asexual)
	•	Limitation: needs genetic data
	•	Example: Using DNA differences to separate species

⸻

REPRODUCTIVE ISOLATION (BIG ONE)

Reproductive Isolating Mechanism (RIM)
	•	Definition: Anything that prevents species from interbreeding
	•	Broadest category

⸻

Prezygotic Isolation
	•	Definition: Prevents fertilization from happening
	•	Happens before zygote forms

Types you need to know:

⸻

Gametic Isolation
	•	Definition: Sperm and egg cannot fuse
	•	Example: Sea urchin sperm can’t fertilize egg

⸻

Mechanical Isolation
	•	Definition: Reproductive structures don’t match
	•	Example: Plant pollen can’t physically interact with ovule  ￼

⸻

Behavioral Isolation
	•	Definition: Different mating behaviors/signals
	•	Example: Crickets with different mating songs  ￼

⸻

Habitat (Ecological) Isolation
	•	Definition: Species live/use different environments
	•	Example: Beetles preferring different flower colors  ￼

⸻

Geographic Isolation
	•	Definition: Species are physically separated
	•	Example: Birds breeding on east vs west coast  ￼

⸻

Postzygotic Isolation
	•	Definition: Fertilization happens, but offspring fail

⸻

Reduced Hybrid Viability
	•	Definition: Offspring die early or are weak
	•	Example: Frog embryos dying early  ￼

⸻

Reduced Hybrid Fitness
	•	Definition: Offspring survive but perform poorly
	•	Example: Hybrids attacked more by predators  ￼

⸻

SPECIATION TYPES

Allopatric Speciation (Vicariance)
	•	Definition: Population split by physical barrier
	•	Process: separated → evolve → can’t interbreed
	•	Example: River splitting a population

⸻

Allopatric Speciation (Founder Effect)
	•	Definition: Small group colonizes new area
	•	Key idea: genetic drift is strong
	•	Example: Island colonization

⸻

Sympatric Speciation
	•	Definition: Speciation without geographic separation
	•	Key driver: mutation or behavior creates isolation
	•	Example: Mate preference differences

KEY HIERARCHY QUESTION (IMPORTANT)

Order from most general → most specific:
	1.	Reproductive isolating mechanism
	2.	Prezygotic isolating mechanism
	3.	Gametic isolation
	4.	Sperm-egg incompatibility

→ Big → smaller → very specific case

HOMOLOGOUS vs ANALOGOUS

Homologous Traits
	•	Definition: Same evolutionary origin (shared ancestry)
	•	Key: structure matters, not function
	•	Example: Human arm + cat leg

⸻

Analogous Traits
	•	Definition: Same function, different origin
	•	Cause: convergent evolution
	•	Example: Bird wings vs insect wings

⸻

MONOPHYLETIC GROUPS

Monophyletic
	•	Definition: ancestor + ALL descendants
	•	Correct clade

⸻

Not Monophyletic
	•	Definition: missing descendants
	•	Fix: must include all descendants
	•	Example from key: some groups require adding extra taxa to be valid  ￼

⸻

COMMON ANCESTOR (PHYLOGENY)

Most Recent Common Ancestor (MRCA)
	•	Definition: the most recent node shared by taxa
	•	How to find: trace branches backward until they meet

⸻

HARD CONCEPT (VERY TESTABLE)

Trait interpretation in trees

If multiple species share a trait:
	•	If from same ancestor → homologous
	•	If evolved separately → analogous

Sometimes ambiguous:
	•	Could be homologous and lost
	•	OR analogous and evolved twice  ￼

NICHE CONCEPTS

Fundamental Niche
	•	Definition: The full range of abiotic conditions where a species can survive and reproduce
	•	Key idea: No competitors, no predators—just physiology
	•	Example: A plant can tolerate a wide temperature + moisture range  ￼

⸻

Realized Niche
	•	Definition: Where a species actually lives due to biotic interactions
	•	Key idea: Shrunk or altered by competition, predation, parasites
	•	Example: Species avoids areas with predators or competitors  ￼

⸻

Carrying Capacity (K)
	•	Definition: Maximum population size an environment can support
	•	Determined by: resource availability
	•	Example:
	•	Parasites → decrease K
	•	Mutualism → increase K  ￼

⸻

SPECIES INTERACTIONS (CORE SECTION)

Symbiosis
	•	Definition: Close, long-term interaction between species
	•	Includes: mutualism, commensalism, parasitism
	•	Examples from study guide:
	•	Legume + Rhizobium (mutualism)
	•	Clownfish + anemone
	•	Parasite in fish mouth  ￼

⸻

Mutualism (+ / +)
	•	Definition: Both species benefit
	•	Example: plant + pollinator

⸻

Commensalism (+ / 0)
	•	Definition: One benefits, other unaffected
	•	Example: owl using prairie dog burrow

⸻

Parasitism (+ / –)
	•	Definition: One benefits, one harmed
	•	Example: tongue-eating isopod

⸻

Competition (– / –)
	•	Definition: Both species harmed due to shared resource
	•	Example: two species eating same food

⸻

Exploitation (+ / – broader category)
	•	Includes:
	•	Predation
	•	Herbivory
	•	Parasitism

⸻

Amensalism (– / 0)
	•	Definition: One harmed, other unaffected
	•	Less emphasized but still testable

⸻

COMPETITION OUTCOMES

Competitive Exclusion
	•	Definition: One species outcompetes another → local extinction
	•	Example: invasive species replacing native bees  ￼

⸻

Resource Partitioning
	•	Definition: Species divide resources to reduce competition
	•	Example: birds feeding at different depths

⸻

Character Displacement
	•	Definition: Evolutionary change in traits to reduce competition
	•	Key: involves morphological change
	•	Example: beak sizes diverging

⸻

KEY RULE (VERY TESTED)

Resource partitioning = behavior/ecology
Character displacement = evolution + morphology

⸻

KEY ECOLOGICAL ROLES

Keystone Species
	•	Definition: Disproportionately large impact despite low abundance
	•	Example: sea stars maintaining diversity  ￼

⸻

Foundation Species
	•	Definition: Species that physically form the habitat
	•	Must be common
	•	Example: coral reefs  ￼

⸻

Ecosystem Engineer
	•	Definition: Species that modifies environment
	•	Example: beavers building dams  ￼

⸻

PREDATOR–PREY DYNAMICS

Lotka-Volterra Model
	•	Purpose: describes predator–prey population changes

Key ideas you actually need:
	•	Predator population depends on prey
	•	Prey population depends on predators
	•	Leads to oscillations

⸻

Population Oscillations
	•	Definition: predator and prey populations cycle
	•	Why:
	•	More prey → more predators
	•	More predators → fewer prey
	•	Fewer prey → fewer predators
	•	Important detail: predator peaks lag behind prey peaks
	•	Why lag: reproduction takes time  ￼

⸻

COMPETITIVE EXCLUSION PRINCIPLE
	•	Definition: Two species using same limiting resource cannot coexist
	•	Outcome: one wins
	•	Avoidance:
	•	Resource partitioning
	•	Character displacement  ￼

⸻

DENSITY-DEPENDENT INTERACTIONS

Density Dependence
	•	Definition: Effects depend on population density
	•	Example: disease spreads faster in dense populations

⸻

Herd Immunity
	•	Definition: Enough immune individuals reduce disease spread
	•	Mechanism: lowers effective contact between infected + susceptible
	•	Key threshold: depends on R₀
	•	Example: measles requires very high vaccination rates  ￼

⸻

EVOLUTIONARY INTERACTIONS

Trait Matching
	•	Definition: Two species evolve traits that improve mutual interaction
	•	Interaction type: mutualism
	•	Example: flower + pollinator co-adaptation  ￼

⸻

Evolutionary Arms Race
	•	Definition: species evolve in opposition (offense vs defense)
	•	Interaction type: antagonistic
	•	Example: predator vs prey toxins/resistance  ￼

⸻

Key Difference (very testable)
	•	Trait matching → both benefit
	•	Arms race → one benefits, one harmed

⸻

WHY LESS EFFICIENCY CAN BE SELECTED

Reduced Virulence / Efficiency
	•	Definition: being too effective is bad evolutionarily
	•	Reason:
	•	Predator kills all prey → dies
	•	Pathogen kills host too fast → no spread
	•	Conclusion: moderation = survival  ￼

ECOSYSTEM PROCESSES

Photosynthesis (Carbon Cycle)
	•	Definition: CO₂ is converted into organic molecules using sunlight
	•	Key idea: Removes carbon from atmosphere
	•	Example: Plants pulling CO₂ into biomass

⸻

Dissolution (Carbon Cycle)
	•	Definition: CO₂ dissolves into water
	•	Key idea: Transfers carbon from atmosphere → ocean
	•	Example: Atmospheric CO₂ entering ocean surface

⸻

Net Primary Productivity (NPP)
	•	Definition: Rate producers store energy as biomass after respiration
	•	Key idea:
	•	Land → driven by solar radiation (temperature/precipitation influence)
	•	Ocean → driven by nutrient availability
	•	Example:
	•	Tropics = high NPP (warm + wet)
	•	Coastal ocean = high NPP (nutrients from runoff/upwelling)

⸻

SPECIES INTERACTIONS (ENVIRONMENT-DEPENDENT)

Conditional Interaction
	•	Definition: Interaction outcome changes depending on environment
	•	Key idea: Same species pair ≠ fixed interaction
	•	Example: Plant + mycorrhizae shifts from mutualism → parasitism

⸻

Stress Gradient Hypothesis
	•	Definition: Positive interactions are more common under stressful conditions
	•	Why: Cooperation increases survival when resources are limited
	•	Example: Nutrient-poor soil → stronger plant–fungus mutualism

⸻

Mutualism (+ / +)
	•	Definition: Both species benefit
	•	Example: Plant gets nutrients, fungus gets sugars

⸻

Parasitism (+ / –)
	•	Definition: One benefits, one is harmed
	•	Example: Fungus taking plant sugars when plant doesn’t benefit

⸻

Commensalism (+ / 0)
	•	Definition: One benefits, other unaffected
	•	Example: Moderate nutrient levels → fungus benefits, plant neutral

⸻

SUCCESSION (VERY TESTED)

Primary Succession
	•	Definition: Community develops from bare substrate (no soil)
	•	Key idea: Starts from scratch
	•	Examples: Glacier retreat, lava flow, sand dunes

⸻

Secondary Succession
	•	Definition: Community recovers where soil already exists
	•	Key idea: Faster recovery
	•	Examples: Fire, abandoned farmland

⸻

Pioneer Species
	•	Definition: Early colonizers; fast-growing, r-selected
	•	Example: Grasses, small plants

⸻

Climax Community
	•	Definition: Stable, late-stage community
	•	Key idea: Dominated by K-selected species
	•	Example: Mature forest

⸻

ENERGY FLOW (CORE UNIT)

Consumption Efficiency
	•	Definition: Fraction of biomass consumed by next trophic level
	•	Example: Higher in grasslands (plants easier to eat than trees)

⸻

Assimilation Efficiency
	•	Definition: Fraction of consumed food that is digested
	•	Key idea: Meat easier than plants
	•	Example: Carnivores > herbivores

⸻

Production Efficiency
	•	Definition: Fraction of assimilated energy turned into biomass
	•	Key idea: Energy lost via respiration
	•	Example: Ectotherms > endotherms

⸻

Ecological Efficiency
	•	Definition: Total energy transfer between trophic levels
	•	Key idea: Combines all efficiencies above

⸻

Energy Pyramid
	•	Definition: Energy decreases at higher trophic levels
	•	Why always pyramid:
	•	Energy only enters at producers
	•	Lost at each step (heat, respiration)
	•	Result: Less energy at higher levels

⸻

TROPHIC + ENERGY INSIGHTS

Trophic Level Efficiency (Diet Concept)
	•	Definition: Energy decreases as you move up trophic levels
	•	Key idea: Eating lower = more energy available
	•	Example: Eating plants > eating animals (energy efficiency)

⸻

Mimicry (Energy Context)
	•	Definition: Non-toxic species mimics toxic species
	•	Effect: Reduces predation
	•	Key concept tested: Consumption efficiency decreases

⸻

ECOSYSTEM-SCALE PROCESSES

Dead Zones
	•	Definition: Low-oxygen areas in water
	•	Process:
	•	Nutrients → algal bloom
	•	Algae die → decomposition
	•	Oxygen depleted → organisms suffocate
	•	Example: Gulf of Mexico

⸻

Trophic Cascade

Top-Down Cascade
	•	Definition: Changes start at top predators
	•	Example: Orca → otter → urchin → kelp

Bottom-Up Cascade
	•	Definition: Changes start at nutrient level
	•	Example: Nutrients → algae → fish die

⸻

Aquifers
	•	Definition: Underground water reservoirs
	•	Key idea: Extremely slow recharge → effectively non-renewable
	•	Example: Ogallala Aquifer (agriculture dependence)

⸻

KEY COMPARISON CONCEPT (VERY TESTED)

Productivity Drivers
	•	Terrestrial ecosystems: Temperature + sunlight
	•	Marine ecosystems: Nutrient availability
	•	Example:
	•	Tropics → high productivity (warm + wet)
	•	Coasts → high productivity (nutrient-rich)

	•	NPP:

	•	Ocean = nutrients
	•	Primary vs Secondary succession = no soil vs soil

	•	Climax = stable, late
	•	Consumption vs Assimilation vs Production:

	•	Stress → more cooperation
	•	Dead zones = nutrients → algae → oxygen loss
	•	Top-down = predators control system
	•	Bottom-up = nutrients control system
	•	Eating lower trophic level = more energy efficient
